What it does not mean

Almost everyone, including people who publish research, reads a 95% confidence interval as “there is a 95% chance the true value is inside my interval.” That is not what it says.

Once your study is finished, your interval either contains μ or it does not. There is no chance left in it. Look at the red lines above: from the inside, nothing about those studies looks wrong. The researchers did everything correctly. They were unlucky, and they will never find out.

The 95% describes the method, not your answer. It says that a procedure like this one, repeated over and over, produces intervals that capture the truth about 95% of the time. Which of your studies is one of the good ones is a question the interval cannot answer.
